## Editorial and visual review

- Each lesson opens with its own complete 2:1 painted scene, followed by the unchanged Saar, three visible narrative beats, the Gita connection, one current-day example, and tappable source links. No new tabs or stage gates were added. A continued verse has a direct door back to its theme story.
- The examples for **guna** and **dhyan** are examples stated in scripture rather than freestanding biographical tales. Guna shows three motives within the same act of giving; the copy explicitly avoids treating people as fixed guna types.
- **Sanshay** is within the Gita itself. The other narratives come from the Bhagavatam, Valmiki Ramayana, or Katha Upanishad, as labelled in the app. No Ramayana/Bhagavatam story is attributed to the Gita.
- **Shok** avoids showing a deceased child and keeps the grief situation dignified. **Kamna-santosh** depicts Pingala clothed and reflective. **Akela** depicts the intact pillar before the violent climax. **Safalta** depicts Bali’s promise without humiliation.
- Every 1774×887 master was reviewed in the 16-scene contact sheet for identifiable subjects, complete edges, useful 2:1 composition, and alignment with the passage and caption. A first Krishna–Arjuna painting clipped part of the chariot; it was rejected and replaced with a full chariot and four complete horses. The final 960×480 WebPs were resized without cropping.
- Final product images are `mobile/assets/reader-illustrations/gita-saar-story-*.webp`. The 16 images have unique content hashes in the approved manifest. All 16 published CDN objects returned byte-identical content in a direct fetch; the publisher verified all 60 approved reader illustrations.
- English and Hindi retellings are editorial adaptations, not source translations. Gujarati and Kannada use the existing app transliteration path for meaning text. Narrative review remains a human editorial gate for public release.

## Source and composition limits

The linked passages are the primary story witnesses used in this pass, in the listed editions. The source list is not a claim of two independent full-text collations for each story. Motives, speech, and sequence are adapted only to the extent supported by those passages; clothing, gardens, palace surfaces, and atmospheric details are artistic depiction. Current-day prompts are authored examples, not promises of spiritual outcomes.
