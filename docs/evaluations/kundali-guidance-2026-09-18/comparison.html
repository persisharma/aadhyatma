Four birth profiles, the previous engine, the new deterministic reading, and actual GPT responses. Two profiles also match official public AstroSage sample reports.

What this establishes: differences in language, reasoning and actionability. It does not establish predictive accuracy. GPT was given our raw chart calculations; the competitor reports are historical natal career reports, not fresh answers to the same dated question.

How the app computes this

  1. Calculate facts: birth instant, location → Lahiri sidereal positions, whole-sign houses, house lords, dignity and current periods.
  2. Gather topic evidence: the existing purpose registry selects houses and karakas. Rules produce support, resistance or qualifying context with basis IDs.
  3. Compose meaning: group house facts into everyday themes, keep contradictions, and show the main theme plus a caution. The lord's actual destination supplies the connection.
  4. Choose practical steps: explicit question context selects reviewed bilingual suggestions. These are editorial prompts, not outcomes inferred from planets.
  5. Preserve detail: expandable evidence and the full export retain factors, sources, dates and the selected question.

What remains limited

Theme copy is still a small phrase library. It cannot learn the person's goals, experience, constraints or actual offers from a birth chart. Karaka factors remain in the full analysis; the new visible themes currently group house-lord and occupant evidence. The headline is not a complete chart adjudication.

Published rule sources still require the existing release review. Agreement counts and theme tones are internal rule labels, not probabilities. Timing relevance does not establish a favourable period.

GPT is a single pinned API run per fixture, with no tools and no access to our composed answer. It tests writing from shared facts, not an independent astronomical engine or the consumer ChatGPT app.
