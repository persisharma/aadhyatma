## What the sources agree on (safe to author as draft)

Panchabali is a set of **five food portions** (ग्रास) set aside before the brahmana-bhojana of a
parvana shraddha, one each for five classes of beings. Every Hindi source uses the same five
names in the same order:

| # | Name | Recipient | How the sources describe the placement |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | गोबलि (gau-bali) | the cow, addressed as त्रैलोक्यमाता | on a leaf, west of the mandala, performed सव्य (thread on the left shoulder) |
| 2 | श्वानबलि (shvana-bali) | the two dogs of Yama's line (श्यामशबल) | thread worn निवीती / कण्ठी (as a garland) |
| 3 | काकबलि (kaka-bali) | crows, addressed as the birds of the dik-palas | on the ground, performed अपसव्य (thread on the right shoulder) |
| 4 | देवादिबलि (deva-adi-bali) | devas, humans, animals, birds, siddhas and all who want food | food and water on a leaf |
| 5 | पिपीलिकादिबलि (pipilika-adi-bali) | ants, insects and small creatures | near their holes / on clean earth |

The sources agree the crow's share is set out first among the "outside" portions in household
practice (roof or courtyard), and that the practice is understood as the **भूतयज्ञ** limb of the
householder's five daily yajnas — which is the hook to the draft `manu-3-70` principle already
in `mobile/src/data/pitru/principles.ts`.

## What the sources claim that we must NOT carry into copy

- "Without panchabali the shraddha is incomplete / the pitrs stay unsatisfied" (Webdunia, AajTak).
  This is a prescriptive and consequence claim; the stance guard bans it. Author instead as
  "tradition counts the five portions as part of the parvana shraddha's bhojana sequence".
- Crows as "messengers of the ancestors" / dogs as "Yama's messengers". Widely published, but
  folk gloss. If used, label as लोक-परम्परा, the way the Karna katha is labelled.
- Any planetary/"pitru dosha" framing. Banned outright.
