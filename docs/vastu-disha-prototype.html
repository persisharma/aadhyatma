Design decisions — frame A

  • साधना group, not ऐप. The More hub already splits into practice (Profile, संग्रह, स्मरण, जप अलार्म, पितृ स्मरण) and app settings (भाषा, पाठ का आकार, विजेट). Vastu is reference practice, so it belongs in the first group — beside पितृ स्मरण, which set the precedent for a quiet, occasional, personal surface living here.
  • Deliberately not a Home launcher tile. This is consulted a handful of times a year — at a move, a renovation, a new murti — not daily. A permanent Home tile would claim daily real estate for an occasional need and dilute the launcher grid.
  • No count badge. Every other row in this group shows state (14 saved, 2 alarms, 3 people). Vastu is stateless — it holds nothing of yours — so the row stays bare rather than inventing a number.
  • NEW badge flows through the shipped mechanism (NewContentContext / addedInVersion), so upgraders see it flag once and fresh installs stay quiet.
  • The second entry point is contextual (frame E), and that is the one that will actually get used — More is the durable address, the muhurat result is the moment of need.

Design decisions — frame B

  • North-fixed chakra, not a spinning rose. The single most consequential call on this screen. A compass app rotates the dial so your heading sits at top; a vastu chakra must keep उत्तर at top and ईशान at upper-right, because that is how every vastu plan is drawn and how the user will map it onto their floor plan. The pointer moves, the dik labels never do.
  • Guidance is keyed to where you point. That is what makes this an instrument rather than a listicle: you turn, and the card under the chakra changes. The room chips let you ask the inverse question ("where should the kitchen go?") without leaving the screen.
  • ब्रह्मस्थान is drawn, not described. The open centre is a real vastu concept and a filled circle at the middle of the chakra teaches it in one glance.
  • True north comes from the OS, and the screen says so. Location.watchHeadingAsync() in the already-installed expo-location@19.0.8 returns trueHeading with magnetic declination already applied — so the app bundles no declination model and owns no correction. Naming the source keeps the instrument auditable without claiming work we do not do. (An earlier draft of this prototype proposed a bundled declination grid keyed off panchang/locations.ts; that was wrong and is dropped.)
  • "श्रेष्ठ" reuses the muhurat tier vocabulary (शुभ/श्रेष्ठ/मध्यम) the app already ships — one grading language across the product, not a second one invented here.
  • The avoid block is the muted terracotta avoid tone, never red — same token as the muhurat "avoid" palette (design.md §60).
  • "यदि सम्भव न हो" is load-bearing, not a nicety. Most people cannot move a puja room. A vastu surface with no accommodation line becomes a list of things wrong with your home — the exact failure mode this feature must avoid. Every direction entry carries one.
  • The chakra is one accessible image with a full narration, and every value on it also appears as text below — the NorthIndianChart precedent (geometry is never the only representation).

Design decisions — frame C

  • The app refuses to claim a heading it does not have. No pointer is drawn, the dik reads "—", and the labels drop to muted. A wrong direction, confidently rendered, in a feature whose entire subject is direction, is the worst outcome available to this PRD — worse than showing nothing.
  • The manual dik grid is not a fallback, it is a peer path. Selecting ईशान by hand yields the identical guidance card from frame B. That single decision makes the whole feature usable (a) indoors near rebar, (b) on a device with no magnetometer, and (c) by someone reading their own floor plan rather than standing in the room.
  • Calibration instruction is concrete — hold flat, away from metal, figure-eight — because "low accuracy" alone tells the user nothing they can act on.
  • Two states, one screen. No modal, no error page, no dead end. Accuracy is a chip on the same surface, so the transition between trusted and untrusted is legible rather than jarring.
  • This frame is why Phase 0 exists. Whether the magnetometer settles usably on target hardware, and how often this state appears in a real Indian home, is a measurement — not something to assume during PRD writing.

Design decisions — frame D

  • A sub-screen, not a third tab on the chakra. This content is not direction-keyed — it answers "what goes in the shrine", which does not change as you turn. Folding it into the compass card would make the chakra screen a scroll dump.
  • Same ◆ / ✕ grammar as the direction cards and the same terracotta for "not this". One visual language for permitted/avoided across the whole feature — and it matches the निषेध treatment PRD-23 uses, so the two features read as siblings.
  • The ancestor-photograph section links to पितृ स्मरण — a shipped feature that is exactly the right home for the observance this placement rule points at. This is the kind of join that makes an app feel coherent rather than assembled, and it costs one card.
  • "यदि पृथक कक्ष न हो" is stated as an accepted traditional arrangement, not a compromise. Most Indian homes do not have a separate puja room; a surface that treats the common case as a deviation is a surface people close.
  • The chipped-image line names visarjan rather than saying "do not keep" — the tradition supplies the action, so the app relays it instead of leaving a prohibition with no exit.

Design decisions — frame E

  • This is the entry that will carry the feature. The More-hub row is the durable address; this card is the moment of need. Someone reading griha-pravesh dates is, by definition, about to arrange rooms in a house they have not moved into.
  • It closes the "when but not how" gap on the app's most-used occasion. The same asymmetry PRD-22 addresses for rites, PRD-24 addresses for space — and this single card is the cheapest possible instance of it.
  • One door, filled, below the windows — never above them. The muhurat result's job is the date; the door is the obvious next step, not a competing headline.
  • Occasion-gated. This card appears only on griha-pravesh and bhumi-pujan results, where placement questions are live. It does not appear on vahan, swarna, or vidyārambha — a door on every result is an ad.
  • Copy is in the user's tense — "before you move in" — because that is the window in which the guidance is actionable at zero cost.

Phase 0 — the spike that gates the build

  • How often is accuracy: 3 actually reached indoors, in a real home? This is now the only significant unknown. A dik sector is 45° wide, so naming one honestly needs better than ±22.5° — and only accuracy: 3 (iOS: <20° uncertainty) clears that. accuracy: 2 is <35°, which cannot resolve a 45° sector. Measure how often frame C is the state, not frame B.
  • Confirm the Android heading path — trueHeading and the coarser accuracy scale both come from SensorManager there, so the trust floor may need a per-platform value.
  • What does the manual-override path cost if the sensor turns out to be unusable often enough that it becomes the primary path? If so, P1 ships override-first and the compass becomes the enhancement — a legitimate outcome the spike should be allowed to reach.

What this prototype deliberately does not show

  • No "वास्तु दोष" verdict, score, or severity. Nothing on any frame evaluates the user's home. The feature states convention; it never grades a house.
  • No remedy products — no pyramids, yantras, mirrors-for-sale, or paid consultation. Same no-upsell rule as design.md §51.
  • No fear copy. Not one line says what will go wrong. The register is "this is held suitable, and here is the reasoning", plus the accommodation.
  • No floor-plan capture, no camera, no AR overlay. Out of scope, and it would turn a reference instrument into a project.
  • No saved rooms or house profile. The feature is stateless — which is also why its More row carries no count badge.
