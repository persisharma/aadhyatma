PRD-46 · ऐसा क्यों करते हैं? — the why behind every custom

Every custom gets its practice, its story or stories, its meaning, and an honest label saying where the story comes from. The answer appears where the question comes up: in the month, inside the observance, on the day, in Ask and in search.

How to use: it is clickable. Tap any question (on the month card, the hub, an observance, Ask, search or bhog) and the reader phone opens on it; page through with अगला ›. Numbered dots are explained in the caption under each phone. Phase 1 = the Kartik pilot; Phase 2–5 = later.

A verse is quoted only when it resolves in a bundled reader, and then it hands off (गीता में पढ़ें ›). Otherwise the page gives the meaning only. बोध: where the popular story and the text differ. The tone stays gentle and specific (“लोक में… ; ग्रन्थ में… नहीं मिलता”). It never says “myth” or “wrong”. This one is checked against the shipped text: the bundled Valmiki Yuddha Kanda sargas 120–131 contain no festival of lamps.

Explain, never prescribeThe stance guard runs on practice, bhav and bodh. It bans अवश्य करें · चाहिए · अशुभ · दोष · पाप लगेगा · अनिष्ट · दुर्भाग्य and “must / bad luck”. A curse inside a story is part of the narrative and is allowed.
Folk is labelled, not hiddenA लोक-परम्परा canon line names its region and no text. It is verified when two sources describe the custom and the story told about it.
Quote only what resolvesVerse lines are shown only when the ref resolves in a bundled reader (Gita, Valmiki). Manusmriti and the Puranas get the meaning only, with no quote.
Reuse, don't retellWhen the katha library already tells the story (dhanteras-legends, ganesha-chaturthi-vrat-katha), the row gives the short answer and links there.
Family practice is rightEvery reader ends with “यह परिचय है, विधान नहीं — परिवार की परम्परा सर्वोपरि है।”
Drafts are invisibleFlipping a row to verified lights every surface with no code change: masa card, hub, observance, today, bhog, search and Ask.
