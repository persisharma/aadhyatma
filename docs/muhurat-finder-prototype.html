Why this is cheap to build

PanchangData gives tithi (with paksha and kshaya), nakshatra, yoga, karana, vara, lunar month and isAdhik; MuhuratDay gives the 8+8 choghadiya, Rahu/Gulika/Yamaganda and Abhijit; kundali.ts gives graha longitudes and the Lagna. The finder adds no astronomy — one new pure module, panchang/eventMuhurat.ts, reading those structures plus a bundled rule table. Rahu Kaal and the day choghadiya are both exact eighths of daytime, so excluding a kaal is dropping a slot, not clipping an interval.

External validation (7 Aug 2026)

The "zero Griha Pravesh dates in the next 90 days" result was checked against published muhurat lists, since it is a surprising claim. It holds. No source lists any Griha Pravesh date in Aug, Sep or Oct 2026; Hindi sources state the bar directly — चातुर्मास … में गृह प्रवेश करना निषेध होता है — and give the preferred months as Magha, Phalguna, Vaishakha, Jyeshtha. Chaturmas start (25 Jul 2026) matches the engine exactly.

Day-by-day against a published Nov-2026 list (engine run for Ujjain; list is New Delhi):

  • Exact match on 11, 25 and 26 Nov — tithi, nakshatra and weekday all agree.
  • 14 and 20 Nov differ by one nakshatra, and both are days where the nakshatra changes within hours of sunrise (14 Nov: Purvashadha ends 8:22 pm; 20 Nov: Purva Bhadrapada ends 6:54 am). The engine reports the sunrise anga by its documented udaya-vyapini convention; muhurat lists report the anga prevailing during the muhurat window. A design finding, not an engine bug — the finder must evaluate angas at the candidate window, or it will disagree with every published list on early-changeover days.
  • 21 Nov diverges (engine: Dwadashi; list: Trayodashi), but that list is internally inconsistent — it runs Ekadashi → Trayodashi, skipping Dwadashi. The engine is backed by in-repo DrikPanchang fixtures and a 150-chart Swiss Ephemeris corpus; the aggregator is not.

Where the draft was too strict: it lifts the bar at Dev Uthani Ekadashi, so its first dates are 25–26 Nov. Published lists resume at Kartik Shukla post-Diwali and carry 11 and 14 Nov. Under a third reading (month-based: only Ashadha–Ashwin barred, Kartik free) even 30 Oct 2026 would qualify. Three attested conventions, three answers; only the strict and published readings give zero for this window, and they agree.

A missing rule the validation surfaced — शुक्र/गुरु अस्त

DrikPanchang states its own method plainly: it selects dates by Nakshatra, Weekday, Tithi and Lunar-Month Shuddhi — which is the draft's factor model — and adds, citing Muhurta Chintamani and Dharmasindhu, that "entry into a new home should not be done while Shukra Tara and Guru Tara are Asta" (Venus/Jupiter combust). The draft had no such rule. It is computable from shipped code — getSiderealPlanetLongitude() already returns Sun, Venus and Jupiter, so asta is one elongation test away. Run over Jul 2026 – May 2027:

  • गुरु अस्त: 15 Jul → 13 Aug 2026 (11° orb) — independently bars the first week of the 90-day window.
  • शुक्र अस्त: 18 Oct → 30 Oct 2026 at a flat 10° orb. Venus is retrograde throughout, with inferior conjunction on 24 Oct 2026 (0.19° separation) and a direct station around 14 Nov.
  • This lands on the loophole: 30 Oct 2026 — the one date that escapes Chaturmas under the loosest month-based reading — sits at 9.71° from the Sun. Barred under a flat 10° orb; not barred under the 8° orb some texts use for a retrograde Shukra. Genuinely borderline, and no published list carries an October date regardless.

Open gates before this ships

  • RULEBOOK §10 content review of the rule tables. The nakshatra/tithi/vara lists here are drafted, not sourced. They are religious content and need ≥2 authoritative concordant sources each, with recension notes.
  • Shukra/Guru asta must be added, and its orb stated (10° flat vs 8° retrograde Venus, 11° Jupiter). Strictly, Tara asta is heliacal set/rise — latitude- and visibility-dependent, not a fixed orb.
  • Bhadra is approximated — detection uses the sunrise karana, but Bhadra spans half a tithi and can start mid-day. Needs a window solver or a stated limitation.
  • Chaturmas convention must be picked and named; three readings are attested.
  • Regional variance. North and South practice diverge, as do sampradayas. Name the convention followed (DrikPanchang, as the rest of the Panchang subsystem already does).
  • Vivah is deliberately excluded — needs both charts and guna milan, and is the highest-stakes decision a user could take from an app.
  • Abhijit on Wednesday. Several traditions void it; the engine always emits it. Flagged, not decided.
