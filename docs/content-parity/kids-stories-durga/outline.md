Adaptation: original editorial prose, no new liturgy or invented dialogue. Battles remain causally explicit; deaths are described without graphic injury. Eight-armed Durga is a conventional illustrative representation, not a reproduction of the eighteen-armed chapter-2 meditation. Children are never instructed to imitate warfare.

## Collection structure

- Maa Durga has the Navaratri introduction, nine form readings and the larger sourced stories.
- Katyayani's form reading opens the same Mahishasura story; do not create a duplicate retelling just to obtain nine separate JSON files.
- Shailaputri, Brahmacharini, Chandraghanta, Skandamata and Mahagauri use related Parvati episodes with the connection explained in their source notes.
- Kushmanda, Kalaratri and Siddhidatri are explicitly introductions when no distinct standalone narrative has been established. No invented adventure or dialogue fills the gap.
- Raktabeej remains a Durga–Kali/Chamunda episode; do not silently rename Kali as Kalaratri.
- New art is R2-served through content hashes. If credentials remain absent, local WebPs, manifests and an upload bundle are delivered; that is not remote publication evidence.

## Durga, Kali and Raktabeej

Base: Devi Mahatmya 7.27; 8.1–63, compared with Pargiter cantos 87–88. Raktabeej is a commander in Shumbha's campaign, not Mahishasura's ally in the earlier episode. Kali receives the name Chamunda after defeating Chanda and Munda. Her consuming the blood and the new asuras is essential to the outcome; the text retains it, while the art avoids injury, severed heads and gore. A small red drop is a narrative symbol, never a substitution with invented magic seeds.

## Navaratri and short form introductions

Navaratri (`nv01`–`nv03`): nine nights of devotion; the traditional nine forms in order; different family customs and child-appropriate participation through listening, singing and kindness. Base: Devi Kavacha's nine names in the Saptashati publication, the Navadurga dhyana compilation and Drik Panchang's traditional Navadurga guide. Do not turn varying festival customs into compulsory rituals or fasting instructions.

Kushmanda (`ku01`–`ku03`): fourth-day identity; the traditional account of her smile and cosmic light, expressly labelled a devotional account rather than a separately verified Purana adventure; solar radiance and eight-handed iconography. Drik Panchang's Details paragraph supplies the smile account; the dhyana supplies an independent depiction/name, not an independent witness of that origin story.

Kalaratri (`kr01`–`kr02`): seventh-day identity, dark complexion and donkey; fearsome protector with blessing and reassurance gestures; Shubhankari, auspicious to devotees. Two distinct pages cover this introduction; do not pad it with a third repeated page. No invented demon episode and no automatic equation with Kali/Chamunda. Four hands, clothed conventional devotional art; the hymn's unadorned/nude description is not reproduced for children.

Siddhidatri (`si01`–`si03`): ninth-day identity; traditional Shiva–Shakti/Ardhanarishvara association explicitly attributed; giver of divine attainments, served by devas and other beings, seated on a lotus with four hands. No promises of supernatural powers to children, no practice instructions and no invented adventure.

## Suratha and Samadhi

Base: Devi Mahatmya 1.1–54; 13; Pargiter cantos 81 and 93. Shared worship lasts three years, not merely nine festival nights. Self-blood offerings and restrictive fasting are omitted rather than taught to children. Suratha's promised return to his kingdom and future Savarni Manu birth differ from Samadhi's request for liberating knowledge.
