# Four illustrated children's stories

Source passages opened on 2026-10-07. Narration is an original, concise retelling in Hindi, English, Gujarati and Kannada, not a quotation of the translations below. The final source note identifies the selected tradition; each page carries a passage reference. Takeaways are editorial interpretations, not scriptural quotations or promises. Frightening events are described briefly, without graphic illustrations.

## Putana

Primary narrative: Bhagavata Purana 10.6, checked against the [BBT edition](https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/10/6/) and [Aadhar's translation](https://bhagavata.org/canto10/chapter6.html). Pages follow the commission (2), disguise (4–9), poisoned nursing and Krishna's power (8–12), death and rescue (13–18), maternal care (19–30), and mercy/reunion (34–38,42–43).

Keep infant Krishna throughout. Putana's poison is applied to her breast; do not invent a poison bottle, poisoned fruit, or a battle with an older Krishna. Her death and the maternal status granted by Krishna are retained. The royal conversation and costume are illustrative staging of the commission, rather than additional dialogue attributed to scripture.

## Kaliya Nag

Primary narrative: Bhagavata Purana 10.16, checked against the [BBT edition](https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/10/16/) and [Aadhar's translation](https://bhagavata.org/canto10/chapter16.html). Pages follow pollution (1,4–6), kadamba leap (6–8), coils and the worried community (9–24), dance (25–30), wives' prayer (31–33,49–54), submission and departure (55–67), and restored river (65–67). No ball-retrieval episode is added. Balarama does not accompany Krishna's initial outing; he restrains the community later at the river. Hoods are visually abbreviated, as disclosed in the source note, without claiming a literal illustrated count.

## Ganesha birth

Selected tradition: Shiva Purana 2.4, Rudra-samhita, Kumara-khanda, Shvetakalpa account. The text itself acknowledges different birth accounts; do not mix the Shani account into this version. Checked against J. L. Shastri's translation: [13: creation and doorway](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/shiva-purana-english/d/doc226133.html), [14: dispute](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/shiva-purana-english/d/doc226134.html), [15: conflict](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/shiva-purana-english/d/doc226135.html), [16: death](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/shiva-purana-english/d/doc226136.html), [17: restoration](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/shiva-purana-english/d/doc226137.html), [18: reunion and honours](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/shiva-purana-english/d/doc226138.html).

The [Sanskrit Kumara-khanda text](https://sanskritdocuments.org/doc_purana/shivapurANam2rudrasaMhitA4kumArakhaNDaH.pdf) independently corroborates the publication: 17.47–59 covers restoration, including the single-tusked elephant (17.49), and 18.3–4 the honours and mother's embrace. This is a comparison of Sanskrit text with an English translation, not a claim of independent manuscript recensions. Before restoration the boy has a human head. After restoration he has an elephant head. Use the source's body-paste account, without asserting turmeric as a scriptural ingredient. Do not illustrate beheading, a headless body or an elephant's injury. Page 4 illustrates Parvati's grief; page 5 shows the already-restored child. Two arms are a consistent storybook depiction rather than a claim about all Ganesha iconography.

## Hanuman and the sun

Selected tradition: Valmiki Ramayana, Uttara-kanda 35–36, checked against Hari Prasad Shastri's translation: [35: flight, Indra and Vayu](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-ramayana-of-valmiki/d/doc424801.html), [36: revival and blessings](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-ramayana-of-valmiki/d/doc424802.html). Child Hanuman mistakes the rising sun for fruit. Vayu cools and protects him. Rahu seeks Indra; Hanuman also approaches Airavata; the thunderbolt injures him. Brahma restores him; Vayu releases the air. The gods grant protection and learning.

The Sanskrit [sarga 35](https://sanskritdocuments.org/mirrors/ramayana/utf/7_uttarakanda_35.html) and [sarga 36](https://sanskritdocuments.org/mirrors/ramayana/utf/7_uttarakanda_36.html) corroborate the English edition. The Sanskrit e-text credits Tokunaga's digital text and John Smith's revised version. Airavata has four tusks (35.37); Brahma's touch and boons follow 36.3–24. Do not say that this account describes swallowing the sun. The ending foreshadows serving Rama, as chapter 36 does; it does not jump into a separate adult adventure. Keep young Hanuman's age, clothing and appearance consistent. Thunderbolt injury is expressed without visible wounds.
