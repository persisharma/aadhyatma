# Source and adaptation review

Reviewed 2026-10-11. Base: Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda, 77-sarga sequence. This original Hindi retelling compresses lists and repetitions while preserving the principal causes, responses and consequences across the full arc.

## Evidence actually used

1. [Valmikiramayan.net Bala Kanda](https://www.valmikiramayan.net/utf8/baala/sarga1/balaroman1.htm): Sanskrit, transliteration, English word meanings and commentary. All 77 chapter introductions/arc descriptions consulted; critical narrative body passages checked separately.
2. [RamCharit.in Bala Kanda index](https://www.ramcharit.in/valmiki-ramayana-bala-kanda-in-sanskrit-with-hindi-meaning-pdf/): all 77 indexed topics mapped; Hindi/Sanskrit chapter pages fetched and selected critical passages compared.
3. `mobile/src/data/valmiki-ramayan/chapter-01.json`: existing derived corpus, 77 sargas and 2,217 local verse records. This is a navigation/comparison aid, not an independent third publication. Chapter identity comes from the `reference` field; the local `stanza` field is unsuitable for grouping. Individual local translated verse alignment may differ.

The fetch manifest proves availability and hashes, not that every Sanskrit verse received independent editorial comparison. The full 137-panel Hindi text and every adjacent transition received an authoring pass. Independent complete Sanskrit/Hindi review remains pending. No claims of translation parity are made for unauthored locales.

## Decisions affecting the retelling

| Chapters | Decision |
|---|---|
| 1–4 | Narada's overview and the later Lava/Kusha recitation are framing, not evidence that the journey's Rama is an older king. |
| 8–17 | Anga invitation precedes Rishyasringa's royal rites; the visiting women are not the three queens. Ashvamedha and the progeny rite remain distinguishable. |
| 18–22 | Exactly four brothers; adolescent Rama's under-sixteen statement is retained without inventing exact later ages. |
| 23–30 | Kamadeva's consequence, Tataka's threat/protection dilemma, weapon recall, Vamana and the distinct fates of Maricha/Subahu retained. |
| 31–47 | The journey's embedded legends preserve their framing. Bhagiratha, not Sagara, finally brings Ganga. Six Krittikas/six-faced Kartikeya and seven Maruts are distinguished. The churning treasures follow this passage rather than importing a later standard list. |
| 48–49 | Ahalya knows Indra's identity in this account; her penance is unseen, not a stone transformation. Hospitality and reunion close the episode. Sexual detail is rendered modestly. |
| 51–65 | Shatananda narrates Vishwamitra's earlier life. Royal ambition, Shabala's refusal, retaliation, failed force, Trishanku, Shunahshepa and overcoming anger retain cause and consequence. No skin/caste caricature illustrates Trishanku's curse. |
| 61–62 | Ambarisha's missing animal is called यज्ञपशु, not automatically a horse. Shunahshepa's peril, appeal, prayers and Indra's boon are distinct steps; the violent act is not graphically depicted. |
| 66–73 | Infant Sita's furrow origin is retrospective. Earlier kings failed; an added Ravana bow-contest is not inserted. Correct four pairs: Rama–Sita, Lakshmana–Urmila, Bharata–Mandavi, Shatrughna–Shrutakirti. |
| 74–77 | Parashurama's Vaishnava bow is separate from the broken Shiva bow and remains intact. The choice is mobility or earned realms. Varuna receives the bow; domestic/public duties close Bala, without adding exile or coronation. |

Hindi chapter 67 uses a misleading publication slug ending `chapter-65-slokas-with-hindi-meaning-2/`; the index and body identify the actual chapter. The manifest records this discrepancy. Modern website commentary and visitor comments are not treated as scriptural events. Modern translations have not been copied wholesale into the package.

Model face shapes, garment palettes, relative heights and optional iconic accessories are art direction, not claims about exact scriptural anatomy, age or costume. The story does not depend on those artistic choices being historical facts.
