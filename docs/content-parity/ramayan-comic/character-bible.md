# Ramayan character continuity

This is the working art direction for the approved richer-colour comic. Character models require user acceptance; source notes establish narrative constraints, not historical portraits. Blue skin, tilak designs, palette, facial geometry, relative heights, cloth cuts and most ornaments are editorial choices. Do not describe them as literal scriptural specifications.

## How a panel is specified

Select six independent fields: **identity → life stage → dress → form/disguise → emotion/condition → props**. A new kāṇḍa does not reset the face or automatically add years. A flashback selects an earlier event state. A temporary transformation keeps the same identity. Fear, fasting, sorrow or battle strain must not generate an unrelated or abruptly elderly face.

Example: Hanuman at his first approach to Rama is `hanuman-adult` + `ascetic-disguise`, with no visible tail. The same identity later appears as a vanara. Sita in captivity uses her adult facial model with worn yellow clothing and one braid; distress changes condition, not age. Rama's crown is enabled at coronation, not throughout the war.

For every new image, supply the selected master sheet and the closest approved scene. Keep those references versioned; record a new hash when a model changes and identify dependent covers/panels for review. Do not regenerate characters independently from their names alone. This reduces drift; manual review remains necessary.

## Age and appearance across the seven kāṇḍas

| Kāṇḍa | Continuity rule |
|---|---|
| Bāla | Birth → childhood → adolescent journey and Mithila sequence. Rama is described as under sixteen at the Vishwamitra request (1.20.2); no invented exact age is printed. Do not use the adult exile/king model on a youthful journey cover. Sages' own earlier-life accounts need separate flashback states. |
| Ayodhya | Later court appearance → departure clothes → matted-hair forest appearance. Keep the same face across the departure sequence; Guha's banyan latex marks the hair transition. Bharata's Nandigrama regency is a separate ascetic appearance. |
| Aranya | Continue the forest states. Track Sita's garments/ornaments through abduction rather than copying a universal costume. Maricha's deer and Ravana's ascetic disguise are form states, not ages. |
| Kishkindha | Rama/Lakshmana keep their forest identities. Hanuman first approaches in ascetic guise, then returns to his own form. Sugriva's restoration changes royal dress/status, not his face. |
| Sundara | Adult Hanuman varies scale according to the action. Sita's worn yellow cloth, single braid and distress are condition changes. The signet ring appears only in its sourced handover sequence. |
| Yuddha / Lanka | Keep exile appearance through the war; track damage, weapons and any sourced chariot/equipment by scene. After return, Rama's locks are disentangled and royal dress/crown adopted at coronation. In the local corpus this is chapter 6, sarga 131; the consulted web translation numbers it 128. |
| Uttara | Later reign/hermitage states, the twins' birth, childhood and eventual installation as rulers require event chronology. Childhood Hanuman accounts are flashbacks. Neither Rama nor Sita becomes automatically grey-haired because the kāṇḍa changes. The child Lava/Kusha sheet cannot illustrate their later rulership. |

## Sources and interpretive limits

The working baseline is Valmiki Ramayana. The supplied shelf uses “Lanka” for the sixth book; the Valmiki corpus uses “Yuddha.” Retain both as display naming choices with the source tradition identified. Do not silently combine Ramcharitmanas episodes, popular iconography or regional retellings with Valmiki chronology. Any added tradition needs an explicit adaptation note.

The [source manifest](source-manifest.json) separates live-opened published passages from local-corpus-only evidence and artistic decisions. The local corpus and the websites it derives from are not independent confirmations. The second-publication comparison is complete only for the limited facts marked as such; remaining fact/edition checks, content rights and all four complete narratives are pending before release. An inaccessible IIT page is recorded as unavailable, not consulted evidence.

Sita's exile outfit is deliberately a flexible simple-cloth proposal. The departure passage describes bark fastened over silk, and surrounding objections matter; do not turn that moment into “Sita always wore bark.” Her captivity passage also discusses surviving ornament traces: reduced adornment is safer than a blanket claim of no jewellery.

## Panel and cover review

1. Read the scene's source, caption and adjacent beats. Verify who is present and why the action happens.
2. Resolve each character's stage and transition boundary; identify flashbacks explicitly.
3. Compare front/profile facial landmarks to the master and nearest accepted panel. Check complexion under different lighting, hairline and silhouette.
4. Check wardrobe, crown permission, disguise, scale, hand/foot anatomy, tails and required arm/head counts. Props are scene-owned: Rama's bow is not automatically Shiva's bow; its intact/broken state must match the event.
5. Verify all required figures and actions fit inside the retained painted bounds. Preserve complete art and readable translated text; allow scrolling for longer content. Do not crop to a caption quota.
6. Save the final scene hash, reviewed frame, reference versions and dated visual decision. Review covers with the same rigour as interiors.

Future app balloons/captions remain native text. Per-language line breaking, balloon size/position, panel reading order, font support, accessibility and Standard/Large layout must be checked. The English labels burned into this reference book are authoring metadata and will not be reused as reader content.

## Character refinement — 2026-10-11

The user found Rama and Lakshmana too similar apart from colour. The revised templates distinguish facial geometry, eyes, nose, lips, hairline and body silhouette, while retaining family resemblance and age progression. The book provides a greyscale comparison of both full sheets; this is a review aid, not a replacement for manual face matching.

Hanuman’s revised model has warmer ivory fur, cleaner shapes, an expressive short vanara muzzle and an upright athletic build. At the user’s explicit request, adult and enlarged forms now wear matching gold mukuts and carry complete gold gadas. These are iconic accessory studies. Do not use them as evidence that every sourced episode specifies that costume; the childhood and human ascetic-disguise references remain appropriate to their states. Older sheets are retained as revision history and no longer selected in the book.
