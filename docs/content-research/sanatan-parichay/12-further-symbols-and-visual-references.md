## Attributes and mounts: reuse relationships

Museum objects can ground visual recognition, while texts may prescribe a particular icon. Examples already documented in this pack include:

- Shiva Nataraja: drum, fire and dancing composition in the Chola object.
- Saraswati: vina and manuscript in the Bengal object.
- Karttikeya: peacock/rooster context in the Gandhara shrine panel.
- Ganga: makara in the National Museum object.
- Vishnu: conch, discus, mace and lotus in museum teaching.
- Dikpal: guardian-specific comparison in the dedicated dossier.

These are image-recognition relationships, not a complete universal artwork manifest. Do not infer the correct arm count, colour, consort, weapon position and mount for an image by collecting attributes from different centuries.

## Artwork evidence template

For each future illustration record: entity/form; selected source and verse or accession; region/period or living tradition; posture; number of heads and arms; hand-by-hand attribute mapping; mount; companions; colours if established; what is omitted; reviewer; rights status.

Use exact diagrams for directions, list membership and geometry. Generated painting can decorate a reviewed scene; it cannot be the source of factual relationships. No artwork has been commissioned or cleared in this research.

## Diagram limits

The Shri Yantra article is an educational art reference. An exact ritual diagram requires verified geometry and a tradition-specific text. Do not turn decorative interlocking triangles into a “ready for puja” asset. A north-up eight-direction wheel likewise cannot place above and below as two extra compass bearings.
