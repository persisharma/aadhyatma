# Thirty three devata research dossier

Status: research draft, not active app content. Date: 2 November 2026.

## Main finding

There is a source-supported account of thirty-three deities, but no single list can be substituted into every scripture. The chosen Brihadaranyaka passage says thirty-three; the word koti does not occur in its 3.9.2 enumeration. A dictionary explanation of koti cannot by itself establish the intended meaning of a different popular expression.

## Brihadaranyaka reading

The dialogue in 3.9.1 proceeds through several answers, including thirty-three, six, three and one. It is a philosophical investigation of manifestations and their basis, not a census of all Hindu worship. Read 3.9.2 with 3.9.3–9.

| Group | What this passage supplies | Editorial consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Eight Vasus, 3.9.3 | Agni, Prithivi, Vayu, Antariksha, Aditya, Dyau, Chandramas and Nakshatras | Eight named cosmic supports; distinguish atmosphere from heaven |
| Eleven Rudras, 3.9.4 | Ten pranas and atman as the eleventh | Do not invent eleven individual deity biographies |
| Twelve Adityas, 3.9.5 | The twelve months | Do not insert a later twelve-name list here |
| Indra, 3.9.6 | Thunder/thunderbolt interpretation | Keep the ritual-philosophical context |
| Prajapati, 3.9.6 | Yajna, elaborated through its ritual means | Do not turn this ancient explanation into a current household ritual requirement |

The wording prana and atman needs care. Madhavananda's translation/commentary explains the Rudras through the organs and mind; Muller retains a different wording around vital breaths and atman. Store text, translation and commentary as separate layers. The source provides a counted structure, not thirty-three fully individualized mythological profiles.

## Named Puranic lists

The following names were checked in Taylor's Vishnu Purana Book One, chapter 15. They are reference data for that edition, not a replacement for Brihadaranyaka.

| Group | Names |
| --- | --- |
| Vasus, 1.15.110 | Apa, Dhruva, Soma, Dhara, Anila, Anala, Pratyusha, Prabhasa |
| Rudras, 1.15.122–123 | Hara, Bahurupa, Tryambaka, Aparajita, Vrishakapi, Shambhu, Kapardin, Raivata, Mrigavyadha, Sharva, Kapalin |
| Adityas, 1.15.130–131 | Vishnu, Indra, Aryaman, Dhatri, Tvashtri, Pushan, Vivasvat, Savitr, Mitra, Varuna, Amsha, Bhaga |

Edition note: another Vishnu Purana commentary surfaced Dharma where Taylor reads Dhara among the Vasus. Its full textual collation is pending. Do not silently normalize the difference. Likewise, names in other Puranas or manvantaras require their own roster. Use Savitr (सवितृ or सविता) for this deity; do not confuse that identity with the goddess or heroine named Savitri.

## Koti terminology

The Apte section of the consulted dictionary gives both a crore and a class/department among the senses of koti. Those senses do not prove that every occurrence means either one. A commonly circulated explanation such as “33 categories, never 33 crore” needs a cited textual context rather than a dictionary shortcut. [KOTI-APTE](sources.md#koti-apte)

## English draft opening

**Who are the thirty-three devata?**

A dialogue in the Brihadaranyaka Upanishad presents thirty-three deities and then explains their groups philosophically. Other texts use different enumerations. Read each account with its own passage and tradition, rather than treating it as a universal roster.

## Questions supported by this research

- Are the Upanishad's Adityas presented as twelve months? Yes, in 3.9.5.
- Does 3.9.2 say koti? No, that word is absent from the checked enumeration.
- Is every Puranic Rudra name an entry supplied by 3.9.4? No.
- Do all checked texts use the same final two? No.
- Does a different list automatically mean an error? No; compare text, recension and context first.
