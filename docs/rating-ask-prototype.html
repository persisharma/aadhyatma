Three ways to ask for a rating without shipping another popup

The rating ask is currently a centered modal with five stars. It works, it's throttled, and it is exactly the thing every other app does. Below are two alternatives built out of Vedansh's own vocabulary — the completion seal, the flower shower, the diya — plus the shipped modal as a control. Pick one, or tell me what's wrong with all three.

WHERE WE ARE

What's on the branch right now

Honest starting point: this is real, tested, and pushed. Two lifetime asks, a 10-day gap, and an engagement floor of 5 opens / 3 active days / 20 verses. The mechanics are sound. The feeling is the problem — a dimmed screen and a star row is the visual grammar of a system dialog, which is the one register this app never otherwise uses.

Variant C · shipped

The star modal

Centered card, dimmed backdrop, decorative star row, two exits. It interrupts whatever you were doing 2.5 seconds after launch — usually mid-Home, occasionally mid-reading.

It is well-behaved for what it is. But nothing about it could only belong to Vedansh: swap the wordmark and it's any app on the store.

Now on a 5-day loop with no off switch. Since this prototype was first drawn, the lifetime cap was removed and the permanent opt-out button deleted — so a user who never rates sees this card every 5 days indefinitely. That raises the stakes on the questions below considerably.

Strength
Impossible to miss. Reaches every user, including pure readers who never build a routine.
Cost
Interrupts by design. Reads as a system dialog in an app that has no other dialogs.

THE ALTERNATIVES

Two asks made of things the app already has

Both drop the overlay entirely. The insight is that Vedansh already produces moments of genuine warmth — the pushpa-varsha flower shower when a routine completes, the lotus "पूर्ण" seal, the streak on the Sadhak profile. An ask that rides one of those moments doesn't need to manufacture goodwill; it just has to not squander it.

Variant A · recommended

The blessing card आशीर्वाद

Inline, not overlaid. When today's practice completes, a card appears in the page flow beneath the completion seal. It cannot interrupt — it waits to be scrolled to.

It opens with their numbers in Devanagari numerals, not our request. Then it inverts the transaction: we're not begging a favour, they're extending a blessing to a service. The primary action names its destination and shows it leaves the app (↗) — no coyness about where the tap goes.

Only two actions. An inline card that never blocks doesn't need a permanent "don't ask again" — dismissing costs one tap, and the 2-ask ceiling still applies.

Strength
Zero interruption. Warmth is borrowed from a real achievement rather than asserted by copy.
Cost
Trigger is routine completion — so a user who only reads and never builds a routine never sees it. Needs a backstop (see the open question below).

Variant B · alternative

The lamp दीप

A slim strip at the end of a finished text, where the next-chapter card already lives. Beat one offers nothing but a small ritual: light a lamp for the reading you just completed. The flame appears, and only then does the strip open into the ask.

It's the most distinctly Vedansh of the three, and the most delightful. It is also the one with a real integrity risk, which is why it isn't my recommendation.

Strength
On-theme to the point of being unrepeatable elsewhere. Fires at a true completion, in the reader where attention already is.
Cost
Beat one doesn't disclose that beat two is a store ask — that edges toward a bait-and-switch. Only survives if lighting the lamp is genuinely its own reward and the ask is visibly a separate, skippable choice.

SIDE BY SIDE

What each direction costs

Direction Interrupts? Trigger Who it reaches Main risk
A · आशीर्वाद Never — inline Day's practice completes Routine users only Misses pure readers; "blessing" framing could read as emotional leverage
B · दीप Never — inline Last verse of a text Anyone who finishes a reading Two-beat reveal edges toward bait-and-switch
C · modal (shipped) Yes, by design 2.5s after a qualifying launch Everyone Generic; reads as a system dialog

Worth naming: A and B aren't strictly better — they trade reach for grace. C catches every qualifying user; A only catches people who keep a routine. If reach matters more than register, C with warmer styling is a legitimate answer, and I'd rather hear that now than after building A.

Your turn

Five questions I actually need answered

Pick a direction, answer whichever questions you have a view on, then copy your response and drop it in the thread. Blunt is useful; "all three are wrong" is a valid answer.

  1. 01 Is a completed sadhana the right moment to ask — or does putting a store request next to the completion seal cheapen the seal? This is the load-bearing question. If the moment is wrong, variant A dies.
  2. 02 Does framing it as आशीर्वाद read as warm, or as emotional leverage? A devotional register can dignify an ask or weaponise it. I can't judge my own copy here.
  3. 03 Should a user who only reads — never builds a routine — ever be asked? If yes, A needs a backstop: either keep C as a fallback for non-routine users, or place A on the Sadhak Profile too.
  4. 04 Their stats in Devanagari numerals — २१ दिन · ३४० श्लोक — delightful, or harder to read than 21 / 340? The app uses Devanagari numerals in verse pills already, but never for personal stats.
  5. 05 The card now repeats every 5 days forever, with no way for a user to turn it off. Do we ship that, or restore an exit? Three exits are available: a finite cap, a Settings-side "don't ask for ratings" row, or the OS-throttled native review sheet. Also a store-review exposure — Apple directs developers to the native API and disallows custom review prompts.
