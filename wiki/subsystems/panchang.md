## Summary

The Panchang tab provides a Hindu-calendar almanac: date picker, festival/vrat observances, a katha library, Daily Muhurat, private Pitru Smaran, Kundali, deterministic Daily Rashifal, private Guna Milan, and newborn-private Namkaran. Panchang's top selector has three peer modes: `Panchang | Vrat & Parv | Jyotish`. The Jyotish engines extend the existing astronomy primitives and remain offline and deterministic. Kundali uses an Indian birth city for Lagna; Guna Milan and Namkaran use IST civil birth date/time because their Moon-longitude calculations do not need place.

## Details

**Navigation** (`TabNavigator.tsx`, `PanchangStackNavigator.tsx`): tab 3 root is `PanchangScreen`; `MuhuratDetail`, `Kundali`, `Rashifal`, `GunaMilan`, `Namkaran`, `NamkaranResult`, and `NamkaranRashi` are routes in the same native stack. `PanchangHome` accepts `initialTab`, allowing Home's permanent Kundali launcher to land directly on Jyotish without making the calendar root unreachable. `TabNavigator` loads this stack through `React.lazy` + `Suspense`, so evaluating Panchang/Jyotish modules cannot delay Home's first interactive frame.

**PanchangScreen** (`screens/PanchangScreen.tsx`): the three-way mode selector is the fixed first control, so switching to Jyotish cannot move it; the location/calendar-system/My Vrat row follows only in Panchang and Vrat modes. The screen then provides date picker + month calendar + observance list + katha section + a `MuhuratGlanceCard` row. The Jyotish branch is stateful: guests get Create Kundali, Daily Rashifal, and one Navagraha practice card; saved profiles get a daily-first landing with all three guidance rows, a compact Kundali reference, then the same one practice treatment. Focus refresh after save prevents a stale guest landing. Tapping the glance card navigates to `MuhuratDetail`.

**Location list** (`panchang/locations.ts` + `panchang/rajasthanTehsils.ts`): `CITIES` is `[...MAJOR_CITIES, ...RAJASTHAN_TEHSILS]` — 52 nationwide cities/pilgrimage centres (Ujjain first, because `DEFAULT_LOCATION` is `CITIES[0]`) followed by 342 Rajasthan tehsil headquarters spanning all 33 revenue districts.
- A tehsil is any `City` carrying `districtHi`/`districtEn`; the national tier leaves both undefined, and that field is what the picker partitions the two groups on and what labels the row `<name> · <district>`.
- `cityMatchesQuery(city, q)` is the one search predicate, shared by `LocationPickerModal` and the Kundali birth-city sheet: English name, Hindi name, or district in either script.
- Tehsil coordinates were resolved from the GeoNames gazetteer first, then the All-India Pincode Directory, and gated on the Rajasthan bounding box, the district's own office box, and coordinate uniqueness. 28 tehsils are deliberately absent because no dataset row survived those gates — see the generated file's header. `location.test.ts` pins the gates as invariants.

**Kundali engine** (`panchang/kundali.ts`): pure calculations from an explicit UTC instant and bundled Indian city coordinates.
- Reuses `engine.ts`'s Lahiri ayanamsa; Sun/Moon/classical planets come from `astronomy-engine`, Rahu is the mean ascending node, and Ketu is exactly opposite.
- `computeLagna` solves the eastern ecliptic/horizon intersection and converts it to the sidereal ascendant.
- Houses are whole-sign from the Lagna sign. Each of nine grahas includes sidereal longitude, rashi, degree, nakshatra, pada, house, and retrograde state.
- `computeVimshottariDasha` uses the Moon's nakshatra, canonical nine-lord order, birth balance, and contiguous Mahadasha/Antardasha periods over the 120-year cycle.
- `buildKundaliInsights` explains Lagna, Moon, and current Dasha structurally for newcomers; it does not diagnose personality or promise events.
- `computeRashifal` anchors the India civil day at 06:00 IST, evaluates deterministic transit-house support for any Moon-sign index, and returns Favour/Pause/Practice/Reflection copy plus one allow-listed existing reader id. No random, AI, network, or luck-score path exists.

**Kundali hook/UI**:
- `useKundali.ts` owns the on-device profile `{ name?, date, time, cityId }`, strict input parsing, IST→UTC conversion, AsyncStorage hydration, and explicit loading/guest/saved/error states. Empty and corrupt storage are distinct; save/remove failures propagate to visible recovery UI. Birth city is independent of `PanchangLocationContext`.
- `KundaliScreen` starts with a blank city instead of a default profile. Saved profiles open the result; Edit is the only place removal appears. Results lead with `Overview`, followed by `Chart | Grahas | Dasha`, and every tab change resets the scroll position.
- Overview's Lagna, Moon, and Dasha cards are actionable. Traditional rashi labels are paired with plain-English equivalents, while the requested Lagna/Moon explanation copy avoids personality verdicts.
- `NorthIndianChart` renders the fixed-house diamond with SVG and exposes all twelve houses/occupants in one accessibility label; the Grahas table is the equivalent textual representation.
- The Dasha tab leads with current Mahadasha/Antardasha dates, progress, elapsed/remaining time and a current chip, followed by a connected nine-period timeline. The timing summary is accessible.
- `RashifalScreen` defaults to the saved chart's Moon rashi or allows manual selection across all twelve traditional/plain-English sign pairs. It renders Favour/Pause/Reflect with graha/bhava context, keeps the traditional-guidance disclaimer visible, and routes one suggested practice through the existing reader dispatcher.
- `JyotishGuidanceRows` and `JyotishPracticeCard` keep landing/detail treatments consistent. `JyotishShareSheet` captures a 4:5/1080×1350 `JyotishShareCard`: Kundali warns that name/date/time/city are present, while Rashifal explicitly excludes personal birth details.

**Guna Milan** (`panchang/gunaMilan*.ts`, `screens/GunaMilanScreen.tsx`):
- `gunaMilanConvention.ts` is the versioned calculation contract for all eight Ashtakoota tables, score bands, and directional row/column choices; the provenance and supported cancellation rules are pinned in `docs/roadmap/conventions/guna-milan-v1.md`. The B. V. Raman Yoni matrix is directional (bride row, groom column), including asymmetric Horse/Deer and Lion/Buffalo orientations; never symmetrize it.
- `gunaMilan.ts` is pure and calls the existing Lahiri Moon longitude primitive. Inputs are civil date plus time in IST; location is intentionally absent because Moon longitude at a fixed instant is geocentric. An unknown time enumerates every nakshatra/pada/rashi/degree-sensitive Vashya classification crossed during `00:00–23:59:59` IST, then evaluates the Cartesian product. It emits one exact score only when all eight row scores and flags are stable; otherwise it emits a min/max range and varying-koota ids.
- The screen keeps groom/bride as explicit calculation roles and can copy the saved Kundali profile into either role, but copies only name/date/time and never city. Draft persistence is off by default and versioned behind a visible `Remember match details` opt-in/clear control. Draft saves/removals share an invalidating mutation queue, so turning persistence off removes even a write already in flight; never replace this with independent fire-and-forget storage calls. Local diagnostic counters have no birth inputs.
- Exact results expose all eight expandable rows, base score and convention-adjusted interpretation separately. Expanded inputs are localized domain names, never enum keys or zero-based indexes. Range results name each unknown-time role's possible nakshatras and deliberately have no single band or share action. `GunaMilanShareCard` receives a privacy allow-list model containing optional names, roles, scores, band, and disclaimer only.
- `gunaMilan.engine.test.ts` pins every relevant longitude boundary and all 108×108 pada pairs; `gunaMilan.golden.test.ts` checks independently published reports and supported Bhakoot cancellation branches; `GunaMilanExperience.test.tsx` covers both-role autofill, accessibility expansion state, unknown-time range, persistence parsing, local metric serialization, and the share privacy boundary. `.maestro/guna-milan-smoke.yaml` is one platform-neutral flow that must be run and reported independently on iOS and Android.

**Namkaran** (`panchang/namkaran*.ts`, `screens/Namkaran*.tsx`; PRD-17 Phase 1 scaffold):
- `namkaran.ts` is pure and shares Kundali's exported 13°20′ nakshatra / 3°20′ pada spans and Lahiri Moon-longitude primitive. Exact birth time yields one charana; unknown time bisects every charana boundary crossed across the complete IST civil day (including the 107→0 wrap) and renders equal candidate rows—never an assumed noon, rank, hero, or exact-share action.
- The child-mode `BirthDetailsForm` asks only date and optional time: no name, city, saved-Kundali autofill, or Panchang-location mutation. Birth input is session-only unless the initially-off opt-in is selected; its invalidating storage queue makes clear win over an in-flight save. The separate shortlist record contains name ids only.
- Manual browse has three doors. “Choose by nakshatra” uses the shipped Home `CategoryCard variant="launcher"` as a **3-column × 9-row** grid of 27 name-first tiles: the current-language name fits inside over at most two lines, ordinal-number titles are absent, and tile width stays one point below the exact division to avoid iOS sub-pixel wrapping. Selection then shows four full-width `ListCard` pada rows. “Choose by rashi” reuses the identical grid over 12 tiles and lands on `NamkaranRashi`. “Browse all 108” is the explicit long index. Name rows keep the shared `ListCard`, but their 44 pt shortlist buttons are accessible sibling overlays rather than nested accessible controls swallowed by the parent card on iOS.
- Exact results show `NamaksharCard`, a collapsed user-level Lahiri disclosure, lazy corpus-backed names, filters, an id-only shortlist, a 3×3 rashi-sound grid, Namkaran Muhurat navigation, and a 4:5 share model that cannot accept birth fields. Navigation chrome and dense result controls cap their system font multiplier at 1.25 (1.15 for chips) so accessibility text cannot clip through the safe area, cards, or adjacent controls; devotional/reading text remains scalable. The 44 pt shortlist star is a sibling overlay rather than a nested control inside the `ListCard`; selected state uses both fill and tint. Empty filter combinations offer a reset-to-all action, while the approved Ashwini/Chu prototype route has a `Chudamani` row. Name detail remains a bottom sheet and always shows Hindi + English meanings, an attested root/deity link where present, and copies only the candidate name. The corpus import is forbidden from App/Home/root navigation/global search so startup remains independent.
- **Charana is primary; rashi is reachable, not a rival answer.** The charana is the finer cell (1 of 108) and a rashi is exactly 9 of them derived from the same table, so the hero, corpus index, filters, shortlist, muhurat door, and share model all stay charana-keyed. Three gotchas the first implementation hit: (a) the cross-check grid must iterate `rashiCharanaEntries`, **not** `rashiSyllables` — flattening renders 13 cells for Makara, whose Shravana charanas carry the dual ज/ख series, breaking the "nine sounds" 3×3 contract; (b) an unknown-time result must render one card per `distinctRashiIndices(candidates)` — a day covers ≈ 3.6–4.5 charanas against 9 per rashi, so ~1 day in 2 crosses 30° and naming `candidates[0].rashiIndex` alone ranks a candidate the range path forbids ranking; (c) the grid cells are controls, so they carry the 44 pt floor, not the old 40 pt display height; and (d) the cell(s) matching the result's own charana must render as a marked non-interactive `यही · this one` state rather than navigating, or the grid pushes a duplicate of the screen being read. The `NamaksharCard` hero is never tappable (`accessibilityRole="summary"`) — it is the answer, and its names are already in the list below it. Unknown-time `CandidateRow`s **do** open that charana's full result, passing `fromUnknownTime` on the route: that flag (not the basis) shows the "one of the day's possibilities" notice and suppresses the exact share plus its shortlist opt-in, so the range path cannot become a two-tap route to a confident share. The same charana reached through a nakshatra or rashi browse still shares — a table lookup is not a claim about this child's birth. The flag must also travel through `NamkaranRashi` via its `dayCharanas` param: without it, range → rashi detail → tap one of the day's own charanas reopened that candidate as a settled, shareable answer, which is the same hole in a different door. `NamkaranRashiScreen` is the detail — summary card, then the 9 charanas grouped by nakshatra as `ListCard` rows with lazily-counted corpus totals — and is its own route because both the entry door and the result card open it.
- **Not release-ready:** `NAMAKSHAR_SOURCE.verified:false` and the small `NAMKARAN_CORPUS.releaseEligible:false` development shard keep both religious-content gates open. Tests and the release gate prevent the scaffold from masquerading as a complete, attested corpus; customer copy does not expose DRAFT/review/version or on-device/offline implementation language. Automation passing does not authorize release exposure.
- Tests cover boundary/purity/state/corpus/screen behavior. `.maestro/namkaran-smoke.yaml` passed on a fresh iPhone 17 Pro Max simulator (iOS 26.5) on 2026-08-13; Android remains a separately required run.

**Muhurat engine** (`panchang/muhurat.ts`): pure arithmetic — no React, no astronomy, no `Date.now()`.
- Inputs: `sunrise: Date, sunset: Date, nextSunrise: Date, weekday: number`.
- Output `MuhuratDay`:
  - `dayChoghadiya[8]` + `nightChoghadiya[8]` — equal eighths of day/night; the 7-key WHEEL cycles from a weekday-specific start index (day) and `+5` offset (night). Convention: DrikPanchang.
  - `rahu`, `gulika`, `yamaganda` — fixed weekday-indexed eighths of the daytime (`RAHU_SEG`, `GULIKA_SEG`, `YAMAGANDA_SEG` tables).
  - `abhijit` — the 8th of 15 equal day-muhurtas (≈ solar noon); `null` if the day duration collapses.
- `classifyNow(md, at)` — point query returning current choghadiya + kaal for a given timestamp.

**Display helpers** (`panchang/muhuratFormat.ts`): `formatClock(d)` → 12-hour AM/PM string; `formatRange(a, b)` → range string; `formatRangeCompact(a, b)` → range with a shared meridiem written once (`3:37 – 5:13 PM`, full form across noon/midnight — the Home Today strip's chips); `formatEndInstant` → end clock with a short-date suffix on day-crossing. All pure.

**Hook** (`panchang/useMuhurat.ts`): bridges the shared day-store with the live clock. It owns NO cache — the three panchang solves it needs (today, tomorrow, and yesterday for the pre-dawn correction) come from `panchangDayStore` and are persisted, so Home's Today strip and the daily Muhurat card no longer re-solve on a cold start and share days with the finder's sweep. `MuhuratDay`/`nowPeriods` are re-derived per call (pure arithmetic over the cached days).
- Defers both `computePanchangForDate` calls (today + tomorrow) until `InteractionManager.runAfterInteractions`, then yields once more via `setTimeout(0)` — Home remains responsive while an initial scroll/tap is in progress and the hook returns `null` until the solve finishes. `useObservancesForDate` follows the same interaction-aware scheduling boundary.
- `useMinuteTick()` drives the live "now" recalc without re-solving the astronomy.
- Pre-dawn correction: when `isToday` and the clock is before today's sunrise, yesterday's night choghadiya window is prepended to `nowPeriods` so the active choghadiya resolves correctly.
- Returns `{ muhurat, panchang, isToday, nowChoghadiya, nowKaal }`.

**Event Muhurat Finder** (PRD-16 Phase 1; design.md §60, RULEBOOK §17): `eventMuhurat.ts` is pure (kundali-style boundary, source-purity test) — `EVENT_RULES` (6 occasions, **DRAFT pending §10**, `source.verified:false` pinned by test), factor match (nakshatra/tithi/vara) + dosha stack (rikta · amavasya · bhadra[sunrise karana] · panchak · adhik · vyatipata · vaidhriti · chaturmas · guru/shukra asta). `isChaturmasDay` reads the sunrise anga (Devshayani → Dev Uthani), is kshaya-safe (2026: Kartik Shukla Ekadashi touches no sunrise; bar lifts 21 Nov) and **purnimant-normalises** the month so the amanta setting can't move the season. Asta = elongation of Venus/Jupiter at local noon (orbs 10°/11°, validated: Guru 15 Jul–13 Aug 2026, Shukra 18–30 Oct 2026). `abujhMuhurat.ts` re-projects `ABUJH_RULE_IDS` through the **festival engine** (never re-match tithis — vriddhi dedupe lives there) + computed Guru/Ravi Pushya. `useMuhuratFinder` scans ~92 days (extends to ~260 for "first dates after" when empty), one solve per day, chunked behind `InteractionManager`. Those per-day solves live in the shared **`panchangDayStore.ts`** — read by EVERY panchang surface, not just the finder (Home's Today strip, the daily Muhurat card, the Panchang tab, the day detail, the abujh scan) — — RN-free, keyed by ABSOLUTE civil date inside a `locationKey:calendarSystem` scope, LRU-capped at 5 cities (a 6th evicts only the LRU and fires eviction listeners) — and are **persisted** by `panchangDayCache.ts` (AsyncStorage; the only RN-touching module of the set, mirroring `observanceStore` ⇄ `observanceCache`). All three hooks hydrate the wanted range before scanning and fire-and-forget persist after, so re-entry AND a cold start reuse solved days; only a location/calendar-system change re-solves. `panchangDaySerde.ts` tags `Date`s generically so a revived day is identical to a fresh one. Screens: `MuhuratFinder` (occasion list) → `MuhuratResults` (ranked, **empty-with-reason**) → `MuhuratDayDetail` (answer→action→evidence, links `MuhuratDetail {dateMs}`); `AbujhDays`. **Follow & remind** (PRD-16 §6.7; design.md §60, RULEBOOK §17.7): `MuhuratFollowContext` is a SIBLING of `VratFollowContext`, not a reuse — a vrat follow keys a recurring rule whose next date the festival engine recomputes, a muhurat follow keys **one civil day** (`{occasionId}:{YYYY-MM-DD}`, soonest-first, **prunes itself once past** on load and on foreground). The `VratReminderPref` type is imported, and `VratReminderSheet` is EXTENDED (optional `dayOfOptions`/`subtitle`/`dayOfLabel`/`footnote`) rather than forked; the vrat saved shape is unchanged when no window option is passed. Notifications are the **seventh family**: pure `muhuratReminderPure.ts` (prefix `muhurat-reminder`, `MUHURAT_REMINDER_CAP = 8` ranked **soonest-first** — a dated one-shot has no user priority — `ADVANCE_HOUR` imported from `vratReminderPure` so the two evening notices cannot drift) + `muhuratScheduler.ts` + headless `<MuhuratReminderScheduler>`. Follow is offered on the **day detail only** (every result card stays identical) and never on an excluded day; the empty state offers it on `firstAfter[0]`. Contextual surfaces: Today-strip ★ chip (`useNextFollowedMuhurat`, 7-day horizon), FOR TODAY abujh card (`useTodayAbujh`), and an `अनुसरण किए मुहूर्त` section inside `MyVratScreen` (one ★ inventory; its empty state now gates on BOTH follow counts). `verdictForDate` (muhuratFinderScan) is the one shared "grade a single day" path — day detail, scheduler, chip and inventory all use it.

**Anga model — sunrise anga + kshaya capture** (`panchang/engine.ts`): a civil day's tithi/nakshatra is the one current **at local sunrise** (udaya-vyapini convention), with `endTime` bisected forward from sunrise — end times only, no start times (an anga's start is the previous anga's end, usually the previous day; same convention as DrikPanchang). A tithi lasts 19h59m–26h47m, so between consecutive sunrises the index advances by 1 (normal), 0 (**vriddhi** — same anga at two sunrises), or 2 (**kshaya** — the anga begins and ends strictly inside the day, touching no sunrise; kshaya tithis occur roughly monthly, kshaya nakshatras similarly). `computePanchangForDate` detects the +2 jump against the next day's sunrise (memoised in `sunriseCache`, shared with all solvers) and fills `PanchangData.kshayaTithi` / `kshayaNakshatra`; UI shows them as a second row on the Tithi/Nakshatra tiles and as `क्षय` rows on the Muhurat card, with `formatEndInstant` (muhuratFormat.ts) adding a short-date suffix for past-midnight ends. Reference cases: Bengaluru 10 Jul 2026 — Dashami till 8:16 AM, kshaya Ekadashi till 5:22 AM on 11 Jul; Ujjain 29 Jan 2026 — Rohini, kshaya Mrigashira till 5:28 AM on 30 Jan.

**Festival kshaya fallback + vriddhi dedupe** (`festivalEngine.ts` `matchesLunarTithiRuleOnDate`): lunar-tithi observances match the sunrise tithi; when the target tithi is kshaya it matches the day the tithi prevails (sunrise tithi = target−1 today, target+1 tomorrow — DrikPanchang convention; Yogini Ekadashi 2026 = 10 Jul). Month is taken from the kshaya day's own sunrise, EXCEPT a skipped month-opening pratipada (shukla 1 amanta / krishna 1 purnimant), which belongs to the next day's month — using tomorrow's month for any other kshaya tithi misplaces festivals (it once put Holi 2027 on Magha Purnima); the day-month adhik guard must not pre-empt that branch (the pratipada's preceding amavasya can close an adhik lunation). On **vriddhi** (same tithi at two sunrises) the rule fires only on the FIRST day — its evening/night always falls inside the tithi — so monthly vrats no longer duplicate on adjacent days (removed 74 duplicate dates 2024–2031). The look-ahead/behind reuses the per-day `computeTithiAndMonth` cache; the chunked scanner warms one day past both year edges for it. The kshaya fallback recovered ~170 observances across 2024–2031 that previously vanished (incl. Holi 2028, Vasant Panchami 2025, Karwa Chauth 2024).

## Gotchas

- **Regenerate the precomputed observance table after any matching change** — `TZ=Asia/Kolkata npx tsx scripts/gen-precomputed-observances.mts` (from `mobile/`); the app reads `precomputedObservances.ts` for Ujjain, so an engine fix that isn't regenerated never reaches users for 2024–2031.
- **Bump `CACHE_VERSION` in `observanceCache.ts` with every such change** — non-Ujjain cities persist their scans to AsyncStorage keyed by version; without the bump, previously-scanned devices hydrate the old dates forever and never re-run the scan (the fix ships only to Ujjain and fresh installs).

- **Bump `PANCHANG_DAY_CACHE_VERSION` (`panchangDaySerde.ts`) with every panchang-engine change** — the finder's per-day solves are persisted per (location, calendar system) and hydrated on entry. Without the bump, a device that already scanned keeps serving the OLD engine's days forever and the fix reaches only fresh installs. Same trap as `observanceCache`'s `CACHE_VERSION`, one layer down (that one guards *matching*; this one guards the *solve*). Adding a city needs no bump. `dayCacheParity.e2e.test.ts` pins fresh == cached == serialize→revive over a year × 3 locations × 2 systems.
- **`getUpcomingObservances` caps by observance COUNT, not by date** — `.slice(0, count)` runs AFTER the `withinDays` filter. `scanAbujhDays` passed 60, so from mid-Aug 2026 the abujh list stopped at the 60th observance (26 Oct) and **five of six `ABUJH_RULE_IDS` never rendered** — Akshaya Tritiya, Vasant Panchami, Dhanteras, Akshaya Navami and Dev Uthani Ekadashi were invisible for all of Phase 1, leaving only Dussehra plus the computed Pushya days. Pass an unbounded count and let `withinDays` bound it; `abujhCoverage.test.ts` asserts the scan reaches past its 60th observance.
- **अबूझ days must not be graded by rules the Abujh screen says don't apply.** `evaluateDay(..., { abujh: true })` drops the seasonal doshas (chaturmas, guru/shukra asta). It is a NARROW reading and only partly closes the contradiction: per-day doshas (rikta, panchak) and the nakshatra/tithi/vara factor match still exclude most abujh×occasion pairs, so the app can still say "auspicious in its entirety" on one screen and "not suitable" on the other. Deliberate, documented, and carried to the §10 review (RULEBOOK §17.8) rather than silently widened.
- **A muhurat follow must NEVER store its window.** Every window is sunrise-derived, so a stored time lies the moment the user changes city. The scheduler re-derives from `panchangDayStore` each pass and re-arms on **location and calendar-system change**, not only on follows changing — pinned by `MuhuratReminderScheduler.test.tsx`, which asserts the day-of notice MOVES between Ujjain and Bengaluru while the 18:00 advance notice does not. Three siblings of the same rule: prune past follows or the ★ count counts notices that can never fire; a followed day that re-grades to `excluded` schedules nothing and says so in words (§12); and `clampDayOf` pulls the day-of notice back to `windowStart − 30 min` whatever the user picked, because the shipped 07:00 vrat default arrives AFTER an early-Amrit window (17 Aug 2026 Vahan opens 6:07 AM).
- **Home now reaches `PanchangLocationContext` transitively** (TodayStrip / TodayRecommendationsRow → `useMuhuratFinder`), which drags in `expo-location` — untranspiled ESM that Jest cannot parse. It is mocked globally in `jest.setup.js` beside `expo-speech`; without that, two shipped component suites fail to even load.
- **The FOR TODAY abujh card must not lead on a catalogued festival day.** `festiveReminders.test.ts` asserts the row leads with the festival card on those 18 days, so the abujh card slots second there and leads only otherwise (Guru/Ravi Pushya, the common case). It rides the ROW, not `getTodayRecommendationDetails` — that function stays `LibraryEntry`-only so the notification ⇄ FOR TODAY contract is untouched.
- **Do not add a private panchang cache to a hook or screen** — everything goes through `panchangDayStore`. `useMuhurat` had a per-session, cityId-keyed `SOLVE_CACHE` and the finder had an index-keyed `DAY_INPUT_CACHE`; both were invisible to the other surfaces, neither survived a launch, and the index-keyed one silently missed after a midnight rollover. Both were deleted.
- **The widget writer is the one surface that must NOT use the store** — `widgets/planPayload.ts` solves with `civilTimeZone: WIDGET_TIME_ZONE` (its 14-day snapshot is IST-anchored, not device-local), while the scope key models only (location, calendar system). Routing it through the store would alias its days onto the app's. `ScanOptions` declares `civilTimeZone?: never` so this is a compile error, not a silent bug.
- **Persisted retention reaches into the PAST, not just today onward** (`RETAINED_PAST_DAYS` = 2 in `panchangDayCache.ts`) — `useMuhurat`'s pre-dawn correction reads yesterday's night choghadiya, so a today-onward cutoff left Home solving one day on every cold start. Caught by the routing test counting engine calls, not by inspection: the surface still rendered correctly. The second day back is margin for the Panchang date picker. Persist and purge share one cutoff (`oldestUsefulDateKey`) — if they ever diverge, the purge sweep deletes what persist just wrote and every launch re-solves.
- **The day store hands out SHARED `PanchangData` instances** — a consumer that mutates `p` corrupts every other reader of that day (and the persisted copy). Keep reads immutable, or clone before mutating; this is the precondition for routing `useMuhurat`/`usePanchang` through the same store.
- **`panchangDayStore` / `panchangDaySerde` / `muhuratFinderScan` must stay RN-free** — AsyncStorage and react live only in `panchangDayCache.ts` and the hooks, so `tsx --test` can import the engine side. A Jest suite for the cache therefore lives in `src/panchang/__tests__/jest/`: `npm run test:engine` globs `__tests__/*.test.ts` straight into `tsx --test`, which cannot run Jest files.
- **`test:engine` runs under `TZ=Asia/Kolkata`** — the parity sweep asserts Delhi sunrises across a full year, and on a UTC machine a Delhi civil day near the July solstice contains no sunrise, so `computeSunrise` throws. The suite pins the audience's civil timezone instead of the developer's.

- **A `City.id` is a persisted key, never a display string** — it is written to `@vedansh:panchang-location`, to the Kundali birth profile, and into `observanceCache` keys. Renaming `rj-rajgarh-alwar` silently resets a user's chosen location to Ujjain and orphans their cached observance year. Adding entries is free; renaming is not. Tehsil ids are `rj-<slug>`, plus `-<district>` only where the bare slug repeats (Rajgarh, Ramgarh, Shahpura, Kishangarh…).
- **Adding cities does NOT need a `CACHE_VERSION` bump** — the version guards *matching* changes, and a new city has no prior cached scan to invalidate. Bump it only when the festival/tithi logic moves.
- **Don't "fix" a missing Rajasthan tehsil by inventing a coordinate** — 28 are absent because the gazetteer and the postal directory either had no row or disagreed (some postal rows are geocoded onto a same-named town 500 km away, and a few snap several tehsils onto one point). A wrong coordinate is worse than an absent row: the user gets another district's sunrise under their own town's name. `location.test.ts` fails on out-of-state coordinates and on any two tehsils sharing a point.
- **Any picker over this list must virtualize** — the Kundali birth-city sheet was a `ScrollView` + `.map()`, which mounted all ~390 rows and stalled the sheet open. Both pickers are `FlatList` now.

- **Engine is pure; hook is not** — all astronomy and clock calls live in `useMuhurat.ts`. Never add `Date.now()` or astronomy imports to `muhurat.ts`.
- **Kundali engine is pure; UI supplies time** — `kundali.ts` takes explicit dates and coordinates. AsyncStorage, `new Date()` for “today”, and React belong in hooks/screens. The source-purity test pins this boundary.
- **Golden fixtures are independent** — values in both Kundali Swiss fixtures are Swiss Ephemeris/Lahiri references, not captured outputs from this engine. Regenerate the 150-case corpus only with `scripts/generate-kundali-swiss-corpus.py` and its pinned official files; do not “fix” a failure by copying current app output or widening a tolerance.
- **Birth city is not current location** — never bind Kundali input to `PanchangLocationContext`; a user's birth place and present Panchang city are separate domain values.
- **No implicit profile/city** — an empty store must remain a guest state with no preselected rashi or city. Do not hide AsyncStorage failures by rendering a successful result.
- **Saved landing is daily-first** — Daily Rashifal precedes the compact Kundali because the chart is reference material while Rashifal is the repeat-daily action. Keep all three guidance rows on the landing.
- **Sharing has different privacy boundaries** — Kundali includes personal birth fields and must warn; Rashifal must never include them. Neither share card may introduce colours outside the existing theme language.
- **The share card's height is pinned, so nothing inside it may be sized off width alone** — `JyotishShareCard` is `aspectRatio` 4:5 with `overflow: 'hidden'` on a width of `min(334, screenWidth - 2 × spacing.xxl)` (334 on a 393 dp phone, **312 on a 360 dp Android**), while everything stacked in it is fixed-point-size type that does not scale. The Kundali diagram therefore takes the *leftover* height (`kundaliChartSize()` = content height − a 196 dp chrome budget, capped at the historic `min(208, width × 0.61)`). The old `width × 0.61` overran the box below ~334 dp and the `marginTop: 'auto'` method footer — last in the column — was clipped away. If you add anything to this card, raise `KUNDALI_CHROME_HEIGHT` with it.
- **10 pt micro lines need ≥ 1.4× leading and a face that has the script** — three lines in `JyotishShareCard` shipped at `lineHeight === fontSize` (10/10), which slices the top off the line: the header rendered `जन्म कुंडली` as "जन्म कुंडला" and the method footer lost its shirorekha (reported August 2026). The header meta also named Inter, which has no Indic glyphs, so its real metrics came from an unpredictable OS fallback — micro chrome that can carry Indic goes through `pillTextStyle`/`scriptTitleFont`. Same trap as the Panchang calendar `dateTag`; design.md §3.0 now states both halves, and `components/__tests__/jyotishShareCardFit.test.tsx` pins them for all four languages.
- **A large Devanagari line must not pin its `lineHeight` at all** — `NamaksharCard`'s hero shipped 58/78 and `NamkaranShareCard`'s 54/70, both just under the natural line box, so the top of an above-shirorekha matra was sliced off (`के` read as `क` plus a stub, August 2026). A fixed leading is also frozen while the font scales, so it clips again at `maxFontSizeMultiplier`. Give the *container* a `minHeight` and let the line measure itself. Same §3.0 incident family as the Jyotish share card's 10/10 micro lines — and the same fix list applies to the labels around it: five Namkaran micro labels were Inter + Latin tracking (no Indic glyphs, split shirorekha) until they were routed through `pillTextStyle`. `namkaranTypeFit.test.tsx` pins both halves for hi/en/gu/kn.
- **Never put `adjustsFontSizeToFit` on a multi-line label with a fixed `lineHeight`** — the 27-tile Namkaran nakshatra grid (`CategoryCard` `launcherLabelPosition="tile"`) did, and on iOS scattered tiles (हस्त · चित्रा · स्वाती) rendered at a few points beside full-size neighbours, well past the declared `minimumFontScale` of 0.8 (reported August 2026). The platform shrink loop is unreliable in that combination and the failure is per-node, so identical tiles disagree. Size such labels deterministically instead — a fixed size plus `numberOfLines` and a `maxFontSizeMultiplier` cap — and verify the widest string fits at the widest multiplier. `NamkaranExperience.test.tsx` pins one uniform size across all 27 tiles.
- **The Rashifal card has no such slack yet** — its chrome is entirely fixed-height (three `minHeight` guidance rows + practice + disclaimer ≈ 375 dp), so it fits at 334 but is tight on ≤ 360 dp phones. Known gap, recorded in design.md §51; treat the row block the same way before trusting that card on small screens.
- **Rashifal is guidance** — do not add deterministic predictions, random/AI copy, remote feeds, professional directives, or devotional content ids outside the allow-list without an explicit product/content review.
- **Two astronomy solves per view** — both today and tomorrow are computed to get `nextSunrise`. Both wait for active interactions and then run in `setTimeout(0)`; the screen shows a skeleton/`null` until they complete. Do not move Home's Today-strip solves back onto the render path.
- **Pre-dawn correction is `isToday`-only** — the yesterday-night prepend only happens when `isToday` is true; when browsing past/future dates `nowPeriods` = today's windows only, so `nowChoghadiya`/`nowKaal` return null for non-today views.
- **Polar-latitude guard** — if `sunset ≤ sunrise` or `nextSunrise ≤ sunset`, `useMuhurat` returns `null` silently; the UI shows a skeleton indefinitely. Unlikely in practice given the location list is curated.
- **Char choghadiya = auspicious** — DrikPanchang marks "Char" as auspicious (movable/good for travel). Some sources mark it neutral. The engine follows DrikPanchang explicitly.
